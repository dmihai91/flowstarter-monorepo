Inner Work

The question leaders are most afraid to answer

Most executives can tell you their five-year strategy. Almost none of them can tell you what they actually want. Not the goal — the want. This distinction is where coaching begins.

Most executives can tell you their five-year strategy. Almost none of them can tell you what they actually want. Not the goal — the want. This distinction is where coaching begins.

The deeper question underneath the question leaders are most afraid to answer isn't about tactics or techniques. It's about the relationship we have with ourselves — the honesty we're willing to bring to the difficult conversations, and the courage to act on what we find.

Whether we're talking about inner work, identity, or the shape of a meaningful life, the work is always the same: see more clearly, choose more deliberately, and hold yourself accountable to what you said mattered most.

Why this matters

The leaders who do this work consistently — who examine their patterns with honesty and curiosity — tend to find that the external shifts they were seeking follow naturally. Not always immediately, but inevitably. The inner work creates the conditions for everything else.

A practical question to sit with

What would you do differently in the next 90 days if you truly believed that the work you've been avoiding is the work that changes everything? Start there.
