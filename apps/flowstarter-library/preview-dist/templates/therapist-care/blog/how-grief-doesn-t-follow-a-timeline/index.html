Grief

How grief doesn't follow a timeline

The idea that grief moves through stages in order — and ends — is not well supported by research or by lived experience. Here's a more honest account.

The idea that grief moves through stages in order — and ends — is not well supported by research or by lived experience. Here's a more honest account.

Good product and design writing should make the thinking behind the work feel visible. In this piece, how grief doesn't follow a timeline becomes a way to explore how stronger decisions, sharper structure, and clearer communication create better outcomes.

Whether the topic is grief, process, or execution, the through-line stays the same: reduce noise, keep what matters, and make every decision easier to understand and act on.

Why this matters

Strong work rarely comes from piling on more. It comes from knowing what to emphasise, what to simplify, and how to shape an experience so the important parts land with more clarity.

Practical takeaway

If you're building a portfolio, product, or brand presence, treat every page like an argument: guide attention, remove friction, and make the next step feel obvious.
